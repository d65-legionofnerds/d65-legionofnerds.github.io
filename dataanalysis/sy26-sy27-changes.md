# SY26 to SY27: What Changed After Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes Closed

*Legion of Data Nerds · October 2026 · Preliminary: based on the District's October 2026 staffing file, the SY2026-27 state compensation reports, and fall 2026 enrollment.*

Kingsley and Dr. Bessie Rhodes closed at the end of SY2025-26. The District now credits those two closures with **$3.0 million a year** in savings, about $2.0 million of it from staffing. It is using the same method to propose closing **Lincolnwood and Dewey** next. This page compares SY2025-26 with SY2026-27, the first year after the closures, to see what changed in staffing and pay, and whether those changes show the savings the District describes.

## Key findings

- **Staffing fell about in line with enrollment, not faster.** Enrollment fell about 3.0% and certified staff (teachers, specialists, counselors) fell 3.2%. The District's total staff count fell 3.3%, and that figure probably overstates the drop (see [How the counts were made](#how-the-counts-were-made)). The data are consistent with closures helping staffing keep pace with enrollment. They do not show a further $2.0 million in staffing savings on top of that.
- **The closed schools' leaders are still leading schools, and there are more school administrators, not fewer.** Three of the four principals and assistant principals from Kingsley and Rhodes hold the same kind of job elsewhere this year. District-wide, school administrators went from 28 to 31, and principals and assistant principals on the state report from 28 to 30, with one fewer building.
- **Bessie Rhodes's program moved rather than closed.** Its K-5 dual-language (TWI) program moved to the new Foster School. 26 of its 28 certified staff are still employed, most in the same roles. The District's $1.44 million Rhodes estimate counts $1.0 million of that as staffing savings and shows no offsetting cost for Foster.
- **Certified payroll went up.** The state report for teachers and other certified staff lists 21 fewer people, but total compensation rose **$2.4 million**, driven by a median raise of 5.6%.
- **The District's own spring plan doesn't leave room for the October figures.** In April, the District priced *all* SY2026-27 staff reductions, from enrollment decline and Kingsley's closure together, at **$3.83 million** (55.5 positions). The October deck credits the two closures with $2.0 million of staffing savings alone. The actual drop was 45 positions, not 55.5.

None of this proves the closures saved nothing. A closed school's staff can fill vacancies that would otherwise have been hired, and that is a real saving. But that saving appears only as fewer total positions. The District's own counts show total positions falling at about the same rate as enrollment, and administrative positions not falling at all. **The District has not shown its closure savings, and the parts we can check point the other way.**

## Administrators after two closures

<a id="which-administrators"></a>
> **Which "administrators"?** The District's FTE file (used on the [Staff Data](staff-data) page) and the state salary report (used on the [Salary Data](salary-data) page) define the group differently:
>
> | | FTE file (Staff Data) | State report (Salary Data) |
> |---|---|---|
> | Central-office administrators | 23 → 20 | 24 → 21 |
> | Principals and assistant principals | 28 → 31, counted as administrators | 28 → 30, counted separately |
> | IES and other coordinators, union president | Not counted as administrators | 12 → 9, counted as administrators |
> | **Headline** | **51 → 51** | **36 → 30** |
>
> Both show central office down 3. The FTE total holds at 51 because school administrators rose by 3. The salary count falls by 6 because it also includes 3 fewer coordinators and leaves principals out.

**The closed schools' leaders.** In December 2025, Kingsley and Rhodes each had a principal and an assistant principal. Matching them by name to the SY2026-27 state report:

| | Kingsley | Bessie Rhodes |
|---|---|---|
| Principal | Still a principal | Still a principal |
| Assistant principal | Still an assistant principal | Not on the SY2026-27 report |

Closing a school should remove one principal's job and, often, an assistant principal's. Instead, three of the four leaders are still leading schools, and the District's count of school administrators rose by 3. The deck doesn't break down the roughly $1.0 million in staffing savings per closure. A closed school's principal and assistant principal would ordinarily be part of it, and those savings don't appear in the District's administrator counts. The [Admin Growth update](admin-growth-final#october-2026-update-sy2026-27-data-and-the-districts-new-plan) covers the central-office side and the District's claim that administrators were cut 36%. On the District's own counts, the cut is 20–25%, and none of it came this year.

## The closed schools' staff

Of the certified staff listed at each school in the December 2025 staff directory, the share still on the District's certified salary report in SY2026-27:

| | Kingsley | Bessie Rhodes | District-wide |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Certified staff in SY2025-26 | 28 | 28 | 821 |
| Still on the certified report in SY2026-27 | 25 (89%) | 26 (93%) | 729 (89%) |

*Matched by name between the December 2025 [staff directory](staff-data) and the PA 97-0256 reports for SY2025-26 and SY2026-27. Non-certified staff (17 at each school: paraprofessionals, lunchroom, child care, custodial) are not on a public roster with pay, so they can't be traced. Aggregate counts in [data/sy26_sy27_closed_school_staff.csv](data/sy26_sy27_closed_school_staff.csv).*

Staff from the closed schools stayed on at the same rate as everyone else. That is not a failure in itself: a district would normally move teachers from a closed school into openings elsewhere. But it means **any staffing savings have to show up as fewer positions district-wide.** Certified positions fell 3.2%, about the same as enrollment.

**Bessie Rhodes is a special case.** Its K-5 TWI program moved to Foster, and most of its teachers moved with it. Several have the same "TWI" grade-level titles this year. The October deck still books $1.0 million of Rhodes "staffing" savings and $0.43 million of building and utility savings, and shows no offsetting cost for running Foster. A program that moves to a new building with most of its staff saves the old building's costs, minus the new building's. That calculation is not in the deck. Our [SY27 Building Use and Closure Costs](sy27_building_use) page lists "itemized savings from the Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes closures … net of the costs of opening Foster" among the data the District should provide.

## Do the numbers support the promised savings?

| District claim (Oct 13, 2026) | What the SY2026-27 data show |
|---|---|
| Kingsley: $1.01M staffing savings | Certified staffing fell 3.2%, in line with enrollment (−3.0%). Kingsley's principal and assistant principal still lead schools. School administrators +3 district-wide. |
| Bessie Rhodes: $1.01M staffing savings | 26 of 28 certified staff still employed, most in the same TWI roles, after the program moved to Foster. |
| $0.98M building, operating and utility savings | Cannot be checked until FY27 actuals; no offsetting Foster costs are shown for Rhodes. |
| $200K transportation savings | Closures generally add busing: see [SY27 Building Use and Closure Costs](sy27_building_use#closing-schools-busing-costs-vs-building-savings). |
| All SY2026-27 reductions: 55.5 positions, $3.83M (April plan) | 45 positions, and the closures alone are now credited with $2.0M of staffing savings. |

**What this means for Lincolnwood and Dewey.** The District estimates $1.42M and $1.48M a year for these two closures, of which $0.89M and $0.93M is staffing, using the same method. This year's evidence suggests asking:

- **Which positions disappear?** Will the closing schools' principals, assistant principals and specialists leave the payroll, or move into other schools as Kingsley's did?
- **Is the staffing counted twice?** The October plan's separate "Staffing and Compensation" section ($11.3M) also lists 103 positions under "school closures/staffing consolidation." If the closing schools' positions are in both, the savings are counted twice.
- **What about busing?** The deck itself notes "potential for additional transportation costs" for these two closures; our [busing analysis](sy27_building_use#closing-schools-busing-costs-vs-building-savings) estimates how much.

## Questions for the District

1. **Itemize what the Kingsley and Rhodes closures actually saved** in SY2026-27, by category, against the October estimates.
2. **Provide a position-level list of the SY2026-27 reductions,** by school and employee category, marking which are attributed to the closures and which to enrollment decline.
3. **Why did school administrators rise from 28 to 31** in the year two schools closed? Where were the closed schools' principals and assistant principals assigned?
4. **For Bessie Rhodes, what does Foster cost to run,** and what is the net saving once those costs are counted?
5. **Provide SY2025-26 staffing as FTE**, not a count of employees, so the two years can be compared directly.
6. **For Lincolnwood and Dewey, list the positions** behind the $0.89M and $0.93M staffing estimates. Say whether they overlap the 103 positions in the October plan's staffing line.

## How the counts were made

- **Staff by category.** SY2025-26 is the District's table of employees by category as published by Larry Gavin in the [Evanston RoundTable](https://evanstonroundtable.com/2025/11/02/guest-essay-district-65-employees-enrollment/){:target="_blank"} (Nov. 2, 2025, Table 2). That table is labeled **"COUNTA"**, a *count of employees*, and every value is a whole number. SY2026-27 is the District's "FTEs by Employee Category" file (October 2026), a **sum of FTE** with fractional values (for example, 612.83 teachers). A part-time employee counts as 1 in the first year and as a fraction in the second. Part of the 45-position drop may therefore come from the change in measure rather than from fewer people. The District should provide both years on the same basis. FY24–FY26 totals in Table 1 of the same article are whole numbers too, while FY19–FY23 have decimals. That suggests the District may have changed its counting method around FY24, which deserves a question of its own.
- **Groups.** Certified = the CERT categories; administrators = ADMIN, ADMIN SCHOOL, CABINET (called COUNCIL in SY2025-26) and SUPT; all other categories are "all other staff." These reproduce the District's SY2025-26 group totals (761, 51, 569).
- **Compensation reports** are the District's PA 97-0256 reports: [SY2025-26](../assets/Administrator%20and%20Teacher%20FY26.pdf){:target="_blank"} and [SY2026-27](../assets/Administrator%20and%20Teacher%20FY27.pdf){:target="_blank"}, converted by [convert_pa_pdfs.py](convert_pa_pdfs.py).
- **Name matching** uses last name and first given name. A person who changed their name between years would show as having left. That would lower both the closed-school and district-wide rates equally, so the comparison between them holds.
- **Enrollment** is K-8 excluding Park, Rice and JEH, the measure used throughout this site. SY2026-27 is preliminary.
- **Everything is rebuilt by** [build_sy26_sy27_comparison.py](build_sy26_sy27_comparison.py).
