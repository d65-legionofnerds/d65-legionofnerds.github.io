# SY26 to SY27: What Changed After Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes Closed

*Legion of Data Nerds · October 2026 · Preliminary: based on the District's October 2026 staffing file, the SY2026-27 state compensation reports, and fall 2026 enrollment.*

Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes closed at the end of SY2025-26. In October 2026 the District credited the two closures with **$3.0 million a year** in savings, **$2.0 million of it from staffing**, and proposed closing **Lincolnwood and Dewey** on the same basis. SY2026-27 is the first year after the closures, so it is the first chance to compare those figures with how staffing changed this year.

## Summary

**The savings the District presents appear to be the cost of operating each building, not savings already realized.** The per-school "staffing" figures line up with the cost of a building's own staff: its principal and assistant principal, interventionist, librarian, nurse and other site staff. Those costs become savings only if the positions are eliminated from the district. We emailed Chief Financial Officer Eric Miller on October 17, 2026, to ask how the figures were built, and will update this page when we hear back. On the data available so far, **the realized staffing savings appear to be a fraction of the $2.0 million stated: somewhere between a net cost of about $0.5 million and a net saving of about $0.2 million.** Cuts in some roles are worth up to about $0.6 million, but the district added two assistant principals and two custodians. (It is possible that more positions were cut than these data show; we will update this page as we gather more data.)

![Savings claimed vs. likely realized per year, Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes combined: staffing claimed $2.02M, likely between a net cost of $0.5M and a net saving of $0.2M; building costs claimed $0.98M, likely $0.3–0.5M; total claimed $3.00M, likely between a net cost of $0.2M and a net saving of $0.7M.](../assets/sy26_sy27_claimed_vs_realized.svg)

We analyzed the District's staffing reports for the last two years (SY2025-26 and SY2026-27) to see what changed. We found:

- **More principals and assistant principals, not fewer.** With one fewer building, data reported to the state (shared at the September 28, 2026 board meeting) show 30 principals and assistant principals, up from 28. Principals held at 16, while assistant principals rose from 12 to 14. We believe this reflects more schools getting an assistant principal. That raises the question of how staffing plans will change if more schools close, especially if these positions are counted as savings and then not eliminated.
- **Possible double counting.** The October plan counts closure staffing savings per school and also lists 103 positions under "school closures/staffing consolidation" in a separate $11.3 million staffing line. It is unclear whether the per-school closure savings are separate from, or included in, those 103 positions.
- **Cuts for the year appear minor.** Staff fell about 3%, in line with enrollment. The cuts don't move staffing toward the levels in Illinois's Evidence-Based Funding model. They also fell short of the District's own April plan: 45 of the 55.5 positions it planned to cut. Certified staff came in on target (24 planned, 24 cut), but the 12 management and non-union cuts don't appear in the administrator counts, which held at 51. This raises the question of whether the District's forecasts are accurate. (The state pay reports list non-certified staff only when they earn $75,000 or more, so cuts among lower-paid support staff, such as paraprofessionals, lunchroom and child-care staff, may not appear in them; the District's FTE file covers all staff.)
- **No consolidation at K-5.** The district has exactly the same number of K-5 classroom teachers as last year: 147. Part of the case for closing schools was consolidating classes, which would reduce that number. That hasn't happened yet. Teacher reductions came elsewhere: middle school, subject and specials teachers (−13), dual-language classroom teachers (−5) and special education teachers (−2), for 20 fewer teachers in all. We are disappointed to lose any teachers; the question here is only whether the plan to consolidate classes worked.

This raises questions about **whether, and how soon,** the District can expect to realize the benefits it has attributed to these closures, and to the closures it has now proposed.

**So far, we don't have evidence that the closures save the amount forecast.**

## 1. What the District says the closures save, and what staffing actually saved

The District's $3.0 million is the full cost of running each building for a year, itemized line by line in Table 6 of its [FY28 Public Financial Plan Summary](https://resources.finalsite.net/images/v1791591727/district65net/r4rpmppr8puk5ciupn1z/D65_FY28_Public_Financial_Plan_Summary2.pdf){:target="_blank"}, posted on the [D65 Data Hub](https://data.district65.net/){:target="_blank"}. The line items add up exactly to the October deck's figures: $1,008,804 and $1,006,533 of staffing, and $552,021 and $428,533 of building costs.

### Staffing: $2.0 million claimed; net result from a $0.5M cost to a $0.2M saving

The staffing figure is the cost of each building's own staff. For each role, the table below shows the District's figure and how the same role changed district-wide this year.

*"Visible saving" credits every district-wide drop in a role to the closures and prices it at the District's own per-school figure, so it is an upper bound: some of those drops would have happened with falling enrollment anyway. Certified roles are from the state pay reports shared at the September 28, 2026 board meeting. Custodial, clerical and food-service changes are from the District's FTE file, because the pay reports list non-certified staff only above $75,000. Nurses are on that report only above $75,000 too, so their change can't be measured.*

How the total range is built. Some roles shrank district-wide this year (interventionists, librarians, administrative assistants, nutrition and PE staff), but we can't tell whether those cuts came from the closures or would have happened anyway because enrollment fell. The **high end** (a net saving of about $0.2 million) assumes every one of those cuts came from the closures: about $622,000 in cuts, minus the roughly $459,000 cost of the added assistant principals and custodians. The **low end** (a net cost of about $0.5 million) assumes none of them did, so only the added assistant principals and custodians count, about $489,000 at the higher custodian estimate. The actual figure is likely somewhere in between.

Even on that generous reading, **about $0.6 million of the $2.0 million shows up as fewer positions**, or about $0.8 million if both nurse positions were also eliminated. The largest line, principals and assistant principals ($679,030), shows no saving at all; two more assistant principals and two more custodians add cost instead. Net of those additions, the staffing change ranges from a net cost of about $0.5 million to a net saving of about $0.2 million. A second method, comparing total cuts with the enrollment drop (section 2), puts the visible saving at about $0.3–0.45 million.

While the District owns the buildings and leaves them unused, **the likely building saving is about $0.3–0.5 million a year**, not $980,554. The low end counts only the costs that end outright (supplies, copiers, waste removal and discretionary spending, $169,221) plus a quarter of the partly continuing costs; the high end counts three quarters of them. Insurance alone is $376,211 of the $980,554. Unless the buildings are sold, leased or used for something else, that cost and part of the maintenance, utility and security costs stay on the books. The District hasn't said what will happen to the Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes buildings. We have asked the District specifically whether it still pays insurance on the closed buildings.

## 2. What the staffing reports show

![Staffing savings, predicted vs. actual. All SY2026-27 reductions: predicted $3.83M (55.5 positions), actual about $3.11M (45 positions). Closures only: predicted $2.02M, actual between a net cost of $0.5M and a net saving of $0.2M.](../assets/sy26_sy27_savings_dollars.svg)

*All reductions are valued at the April plan's own average of $69,000 per position ($3.83M ÷ 55.5). The closures' actual range is the role-by-role result from section 1: cuts at the two schools' roles minus the added assistant principals and custodians.*

### Cuts mirrored the enrollment drop

K-8 enrollment fell 3.0%. Staff fell by 45 positions, against about 41 that a 3.0% drop alone would predict, which leaves **about 4 positions** beyond enrollment. That matters because the closures and this year's reductions were meant to bring staffing more in line with the number of students the District serves. Cutting only about 4 positions more than the enrollment drop alone would predict is a modest change. We are not eager to see staff cut; we want to understand whether the proposed cuts actually came through. The real number may be smaller than 4. Last year's figures count people, so a half-time employee counts as 1. This year's count full-time equivalents (FTE), so the same person counts as 0.5. That change in method alone makes staff look lower this year, even if no job was cut ([how the counts were made](#how-the-counts-were-made)). At the average certified cost of $115,000, $2.0 million is about 17 positions.

Teachers show the same pattern. On the state pay report, classroom, subject, special education and dual-language teachers went from 599 to 579, a cut of 20.

### No consolidation at K-5

The District employs teachers from kindergarten through 8th grade. We focus on K-5 classroom teachers here because consolidating classes was part of the case for closing these elementary schools. We are sad to see any teacher leave; our question is only whether the plan delivered the savings it anticipated. Closing an elementary school and moving its students into other schools' classrooms is where consolidation savings would come from. Positions may also have been shuffled elsewhere, since cuts can come from any part of the district, but consolidating classes was a key point in the case for these closures. We do see a reduction in dual-language (TWI) classroom teachers. **K-5 classroom teachers held at exactly 147.** Most of the teacher cut came from middle school, subject and specials teachers (−13) and dual-language classrooms (−5).

![Teachers by type, 599 to 579: K-5 classroom 0 (147 to 147), dual-language classroom −5, middle school, subject and specials −13, special education −2.](../assets/sy26_sy27_teachers.svg)

**Why the K-5 count held flat.** 14 K-5 classroom teachers left the group (11 left the report and 3 moved to other roles). They were replaced by 6 new hires and 8 people who moved in from other roles. Six of those 8 came from roles that shrank this year: two dual-language classrooms (TWI kindergarten and TWI 2nd grade, each down one), a special education teacher, a social studies teacher, a bilingual math/science teacher (that title went from 2 to 0) and a pre-K teacher (pre-K PFA went from 7 to 5). One came from an interventionist job, and one is the same African-centered kindergarten classroom under a new title. So the teaching jobs that went away were mostly in other roles, and K-5 classrooms were refilled from them.

![How certified staff moved from SY2025-26 to SY2026-27, by role, with net change: K-5 classroom 147 (0), dual-language 41 (−5), middle school, subject and specials 240 (−13), special education 151 (−2), interventionists and coaches 27 (−2), librarians 14 (−1), social workers, psychologists and counselors 114 (+8), nurse 1 (0), principals 16 (0), assistant principals 14 (+2), central office and other 32 (−5), on leave 3 (−3). 92 people are not on the 2026-27 report and 71 are new.](../assets/sy26_sy27_staff_flow.svg)

### More principals and assistant principals

With one fewer building, principals and assistant principals went from 28 to 30 on the state report (school administrators +3 in the District's FTE file). All four of Kingsley's and Bessie Rhodes's leaders hold the same kind of job this year. 25 of Kingsley's 28 certified staff and 27 of Bessie Rhodes's 28 are still on the payroll, in line with or above the district as a whole (89%).


Moving staff from a closed school into open jobs is normal and can be a real saving, because those jobs aren't filled by new hires. But that saving shows up only as fewer positions district-wide.

### Possible double counting

The October plan's separate "Staffing and Compensation" section ($11.3 million) lists 103 positions under "school closures/staffing consolidation." If the positions behind the per-school closure estimates are also among those 103, the same savings are counted twice.

## 3. If, and when, the savings will show up

Building-based costs turn into savings in specific ways: a position is eliminated, a vacancy is left unfilled, or a building is sold, leased or stops costing money to maintain. None of these is itemized for Kingsley or Bessie Rhodes. Without that detail, the community can't tell whether the $3.0 million is a SY2026-27 saving, a saving that arrives as staff retire or leave, or a figure that depends on decisions not yet made.

The same method prices **Lincolnwood ($1.42 million) and Dewey ($1.48 million)**, with $0.89 million and $0.93 million of it from staffing.

## 5. Data needed to answer these questions

1. How the per-school closure savings were calculated, and whether they are building costs or positions eliminated (asked of the CFO on October 17, 2026).
2. Whether insurance and other building costs continue for Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes while the District owns them (asked).
3. A staff directory, or counts of staff by job title, for SY2025-26 and SY2026-27, so specific moves can be tracked, especially for staff who earn less than $75,000 and so don't appear on the state pay reports.
4. The change in district operating costs as students and teachers from Kingsley and Bessie Rhodes joined other schools, including Foster. For example, the District counts $10,500 of discretionary supplies per closed school as a saving: did that spending go away, or did discretionary budgets rise at the schools that received more students? The same question applies to the other per-pupil and per-classroom costs that move with students.
5. Whether the Lincolnwood and Dewey staffing estimates overlap the 103 positions in the staffing line.
6. SY2025-26 staffing as FTE, so the two years are measured the same way.

## Administrators after two closures

<a id="which-administrators"></a>

**Which "administrators"?** The District's FTE file (used on the [Staff Data](staff-data) page) and the state salary report (used on the [Salary Data](salary-data) page) define the group differently:

| | FTE file (Staff Data) | State report (Salary Data) |
|---|---|---|
| Central-office administrators | 23 → 20 | 24 → 21 |
| Principals and assistant principals | 28 → 31, counted as administrators | 28 → 30, counted separately |
| IES and other coordinators, union president | Not counted as administrators | 12 → 9, counted as administrators |
| **Headline** | **51 → 51** | **36 → 30** |

Both show central office down 3. The FTE total holds at 51 because school administrators rose by 3. The salary count falls by 6 because it also includes 3 fewer coordinators and leaves principals out.

Closing a school should remove one principal's job and, often, an assistant principal's. Instead, all four leaders are still school administrators, and the District's count of school administrators rose by 3. The deck doesn't break down the roughly $1.0 million in staffing savings per closure. A closed school's principal and assistant principal would ordinarily be part of it, and those savings don't appear in the District's administrator counts. **The [Admin Growth update](admin-growth-final#october-2026-update-sy2026-27-data-and-the-districts-new-plan) covers the central-office side and the District's claim that administrators were cut 36%. That 36% is the change in a ratio, students per administrator going from 90 to 140, not a count of people, and the ratio also rises when enrollment falls. On the District's own counts, the cut is 20–25%, and none of it came this year.**

## How the counts were made

- **Staff by category.** SY2025-26 is the District's table of employees by category as published by Larry Gavin in the [Evanston RoundTable](https://evanstonroundtable.com/2025/11/02/guest-essay-district-65-employees-enrollment/){:target="_blank"} (Nov. 2, 2025, Table 2). That table is labeled **"COUNTA"**, a *count of employees*, and every value is a whole number. SY2026-27 is the District's "FTEs by Employee Category" file (October 2026), a **sum of FTE** with fractional values (for example, 612.83 teachers). A part-time employee counts as 1 in the first year and as a fraction in the second. Part of the 45-position drop may therefore come from the change in measure rather than from fewer people. The District should provide both years on the same basis. FY24–FY26 totals in Table 1 of the same article are whole numbers too, while FY19–FY23 have decimals. That suggests the District may have changed its counting method around FY24, which deserves a question of its own.
- **Groups.** Certified = the CERT categories; administrators = ADMIN, ADMIN SCHOOL, CABINET (called COUNCIL in SY2025-26) and SUPT; all other categories are "all other staff." These reproduce the District's SY2025-26 group totals (761, 51, 569).
- **Compensation reports** are the District's PA 97-0256 reports: [SY2025-26](../../assets/Administrator%20and%20Teacher%20FY26.pdf){:target="_blank"} and [SY2026-27](../../assets/Administrator%20and%20Teacher%20FY27.pdf){:target="_blank"}, converted by [convert_pa_pdfs.py](convert_pa_pdfs.py).
- **Name matching** uses last name and first given name. It also links surname changes: same first name, a shared part of the surname (for example, a hyphenated name added or dropped), and no other match on either side. Four were found district-wide, all in the same job both years, including Bessie Rhodes's assistant principal. A complete surname change would still show as a departure. That would lower the closed-school and district-wide rates alike, so the comparison between them holds.
- **Enrollment** is K-8 excluding Park, Rice and JEH, the measure used throughout this site. SY2026-27 is preliminary.
- **Everything is rebuilt by** [build_sy26_sy27_comparison.py](build_sy26_sy27_comparison.py).
